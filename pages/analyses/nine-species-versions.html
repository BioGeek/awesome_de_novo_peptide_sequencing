“Evaluated on nine-species” names at least eleven different sets of spectra. This page puts every public copy of the benchmark on one key, (species, raw file, scan), and follows each spectrum from one version to the next: whether it stays, which split it lands in, whether its label changes, and whether the spectrum itself does. It was computed on 2026-10-20 from the files as published that day; every number is an exact count unless it says otherwise. The code is in analysis/nine_species/, and the catalog’s own record of the versions is the nine-species benchmark’s page.

Four things to know before quoting a nine-species number.

  1. MSV000090982 is not the published benchmark. It holds three builds, from 2022, 2023 and 2024. The published one is a fourth, on Zenodo, and even the 2024 build is 47,640 spectra away from it.
  2. The InstaNovo split, which NovoBench trains on, overlaps the test sets of the newer versions. 17% of ProteoBench’s spectra, 22% of dIon’s test set and 22% of denovo_benchmarks’ nine-species sets are scans from its training split.
  3. One revised spectrum in six has a precursor on an isotope peak. A model that enforces the precursor mass with no isotope allowance cannot get those right; on the original and on the InstaNovo split that never happens.
  4. The original writes every leucine as I. Fold I and L before comparing labels across versions.

A backbone is the bare sequence with I and L folded together, the grain at which two labels can be the same peptide.

The original and the revised benchmark share raw files and nothing else: the revised benchmark re-converted every raw file, re-searched it, and relabelled it. The three MassIVE builds, Zenodo main and denovo_benchmarks share the converted peak lists: a spectrum present in two of them has the same precursor, charge, retention time and peaks (denovo_benchmarks drops a few peaks per spectrum). What differs between them is which spectra are in and what each is labelled.

Where the original’s spectra went

Half of the original’s 1,528,127 labelled spectra are in Zenodo main. Each of the others was dropped at a step of the revised pipeline that can be named, by asking the revised search itself about that scan.

Not one spectrum in main lacks a precursor charge, although 2,345,279 of the converted spectra do and the search ran on all of them: 62% of the human spectra, 56% of C. endoloripes’ and 15% of B. subtilis’. Percolator accepted 75,693 of them and every one was dropped. Wen & Noble do not describe this step. They report dropping “13 MGF files with fewer than 100 accepted PSMs”, and those are 13 B. subtilis files in which no spectrum has a charge at all (103 to 397 accepted PSMs each, and a DIA run with 1): they fall under 100 only once the charge-less ones are gone. Four mouse files with almost nothing accepted leave 339 raw files in main. Subtracting the charge-less spectra and those four files gives 2,898,614, the paper’s 2,898,611 within 3, and the shared-peptide draw then removes 60,497 more. denovo_benchmarks applies the same charge filter when it builds a dataset.

What changed in the spectra that stayed

The original was exported by PEAKS in 2017 and the revised benchmark by ThermoRawFileParser in 2022, and the two disagree about the spectrum itself.

The precursor m/z. Among the 801,288 spectra the original shares with the 2022 build, 15% have a neutral precursor mass 1 or 2 Da heavier in the revised version. Every precursor in the original sits on its label’s monoisotopic peak; ThermoRawFileParser writes the m/z the instrument selected, which is often an isotope peak. The revised search “allowed for 1 isotope error in the selection of candidate peptides” (Wen & Noble, Methods), and the released MGFs keep the instrument’s m/z.

Over Zenodo main it is 484,359 spectra, 17.1%, from 1.6% of V. mungo to 24.8% of yeast; 13.6% of ProteoBench’s. The original, the InstaNovo split and denovo_benchmarks have none. These are isotope picks and not missed deamidations, which sit 0.019 Da away: the mass difference centres on 1.003 Da, the error assuming a carbon-13 offset has a median of 1.0 ppm against 11.0 ppm assuming deamidation, and 74,170 of the offset spectra have no N or Q to deamidate. Casanovo’s default allows an offset of 0 or 1; check what a given model does before comparing its numbers across the two.

The peaks. The original’s peak lists are sparser (median 142 peaks against 334) and reach fragment m/z values no fragment of the precursor can have (up to 47,568, against 5,496). For mouse, where both sides could be downloaded whole, 19,993 spectra were compared peak by peak: a median 97.6% of the original’s peaks are in the revised peak list within 10 ppm, carrying 97.0% of its intensity, and the original’s intensities are about 2.5 times the revised ones. The original is the same scan after PEAKS’s own processing.

Everything else barely moves: 20 of 801,288 shared spectra change charge, every retention time agrees within 1 s, and the revised builds hold no charge-1 spectra (the original has 0.24%) and no peptide shorter than 6 or longer than 50 residues (the original runs from 5 to 65).

The modification vocabulary. The original and the 2022 build carry oxidation, N and Q deamidation and carbamidomethylation. From 2023 on, four N-terminal modifications appear: acetylation (+42.01), carbamylation (+43.01), ammonia loss (-17.03) and carbamylation with ammonia loss (+25.98), on 6.6% of main’s spectra. A model trained on the original or on the InstaNovo split has never seen one.

2022 to 2023 is a re-search. 83% of the spectra leaving, and 83% of those arriving, have a peptide the other build keeps in the same species: different scans of the same peptide passed the FDR. Of the 41,389 relabelled spectra, 21,818 gain, lose or move an N or Q deamidation, 1,226 gain an N-terminal modification, 10,775 move an I/L or a modification, and 7,570 change peptide.

After 2023 every change is the shared-peptide draw. Wen & Noble give each peptide found in more than one species to one species at random and drop its spectra from the others. Re-running that draw moves whole peptides between species files: 28,673 of the 28,674 spectra leaving between 2023 and 2024, and all 20,426 arriving between 2024 and Zenodo, carry a peptide found in more than one species. Backbones present in more than one species file: 855 in 2022, 1,458 in 2023, 353 in 2024, and 0 in Zenodo main. The benchmark repository cannot regenerate any of them exactly: its seed is fixed, but the species and file order it draws over come from the filesystem.

denovo_benchmarks’ nine species

The living benchmark’s datasets file calls its nine-species sets “De novo nine-species benchmark (MSV000090982)”. They are not. Its own deposit, MSV000096182, holds 101 raw files relabelled by its own search: 946,155 labels, keyed as <raw file>_<chunk>:<index> into MGF chunks of at most 20,000 charged spectra.

  • A different subset of files. Mouse takes all 13 raw files, including the four main drops; B. subtilis takes 4 of 106 and yeast 3 of 27; and honeybee includes BM-2, the file the revised search never had.
  • Different labels. Of the spectra it shares with Zenodo main (478,000), 87.6% carry the same label. 467,000 of its spectra, half, are not in main at all. 188,000 labels carry a modification the revised search did not allow: N-terminal, D, E and H carbamidomethylation, sodium on D and E, lysine and arginine methylation, and +12 and +26 N-terminal adducts.
  • A deamidation where the others see an isotope peak. On 44,464 spectra that main labels at the M+1 peak, denovo_benchmarks writes the same backbone with one more deamidation in 39,011 cases. The carbon-13 reading fits better for 37,982 of them (median error 0.9 ppm against 12.1 ppm), so about 4% of these labels most likely carry a deamidation that is not there.
  • A deposit that does not match its labels in two files. 1,014 labels point past the end of their MGF chunk (BC-2_1 holds 18,996 spectra and is cited up to index 19,933), so those chunks are not the files the labels were made from.

Splits, and what a model has already seen

The original folds are disjoint on the modified peptide, not on the backbone. No validation or test label of a fold is a training label of that fold, but 29 to 36% of every fold’s validation and test spectra carry a backbone it trains on, in another modification state. Across species, the held-out species’ spectra whose backbone the fold trained on run from 0.4% (M. mazei) to 22.6% (V. mungo). The revised benchmark was built to remove that, and did: no backbone in Zenodo main is in two species.

The InstaNovo split is the original’s yeast fold, the same 499,402 training spectra NovoBench retrains on: its train and valid are that fold’s, and its test set is the whole original yeast file. Training on it and testing on another version outside yeast tests partly on training data, because the spectra are the same physical scans, re-labelled.

ProteoBench’s yeast and B. subtilis spectra are untouched, but 71% of its mouse spectra and half its human ones are scans a NovoBench-style model was trained on. A number such a model scores on ProteoBench, on main beyond the yeast file, on dIon or on denovo_benchmarks is partly a training score.

What to write when reporting a number

  • Name the build, not the accession. “Revised nine-species” is three builds under one MassIVE accession plus the Zenodo record; the InstaNovo IPC copy is the 2023 build; denovo_benchmarks is a selection of its own.
  • Do not train on the InstaNovo or NovoBench split and test on a newer version outside yeast. Up to 22% of the test spectra are training scans, and about 60% carry a backbone the model trained on.
  • Allow a one-dalton precursor offset, or score the revised spectra whose precursor is an isotope peak separately.
  • Fold I and L before comparing labels across versions.

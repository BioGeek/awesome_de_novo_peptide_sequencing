Chemical derivatization assisted

33 methods · 1999–2026

Chemical derivatization assisted: Covalent modification of the peptide, usually at a terminus, to change how it fragments: selective cleavage, brighter low-mass ions, or a strongly directed ion series.

The earliest of its 33 methods is PSD MALDI high-sensitivity de novo sequencing (1999); 32 more have followed.

Methods (33)

Oldest first, by the paper that describes each one.

  • PSD MALDI high-sensitivity de novo sequencing (1999): High-sensitivity postsource decay MALDI method for de novo peptide sequencing with N-terminal sulfonation.
  • Charge derivatization MALDI-PSD de novo sequencing (1999): Charge-derivatizes a proteolytic digest before MALDI post-source decay so the fragment series reads cleanly, evaluated on a phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase of known structure and then used to sequence an unknown variant from Anaerobiospirillum succiniciproducens.
  • Lysine derivatization PSD de novo sequencing (2000): Derivatization workflow for lysine-terminated tryptic peptides to facilitate PSD MALDI de novo sequencing.
  • N-sulfonated peptide MALDI TOF/TOF de novo sequencing (2004): N-sulfonation before MALDI TOF/TOF directs fragmentation into a single dominant series, so a peptide mass fingerprint that does not identify a protein on its own can be followed by a readable de novo spectrum.
  • CHASE (2005): Charge Assisted Sequencing Engine: derivatizes tryptic peptides at the N-terminus with a negatively charged reagent, reads the resulting PSD/TOF-TOF spectra de novo and passes sequences to BLAST.
  • Peroxycarbamate N-terminal labelling (2006): N-terminal t-butyl peroxycarbamate derivatives lose the N-terminal side chain in CID, identifying that residue and labelling the b-ion series to aid de novo sequencing.
  • IRMPD of N-terminal sulfonated peptides (2006): Infrared multiphoton dissociation of N-terminal sulfonated peptides in a quadrupole ion trap, which both dissociates them efficiently and carries the y-ion series all the way down to y1, so the C-terminal residue is not lost.
  • Charged tag with C-terminal digestion (2007): Couples a fixed-charge N-terminal derivatization with a C-terminal digestion of the derivatized peptide, so MALDI spectra carry one interpretable series and the ladder can be read directly.
  • N-terminal fragment isolation de novo sequencing (2007): Isolates protein N-terminal peptides specifically, through reduction, S-alkylation, guanidination and a cleavable biotin coupling, so several proteins can be N-terminally sequenced in parallel at low picomole level.
  • AnchorChip on-target SPITC derivatization (2008): An on-target AnchorChip clean-up for 4-sulfophenyl isothiocyanate derivatized tryptic peptides that replaces reverse-phase desalting before MALDI TOF-TOF de novo sequencing; applied to proteins from fish liver gels.
  • Lys Tag (2008): A single robust lysine modification before MALDI TOF/TOF that improves de novo readability without a multi-step workup, aimed at identifying proteins confidently from biological samples.
  • TMPP arginine-peptide de novo sequencing (2008): Extends TMPP-Ac-OSu tagging to arginine-containing peptides, whose fragmentation the basic side chain otherwise complicates, simplifying the pattern enough to read a sequence.
  • TMPP N-terminal peptide de novo sequencing (2008): Tags peptide N-termini with TMPP-Ac-OSu after metalloendopeptidase digestion and isolates them, giving MALDI spectra of N-terminal peptides clean enough to sequence de novo.
  • Derivatization plus ETcaD de novo (2009): N-terminally derivatized, multiply charged peptides are charge-reduced by electron transfer and then collisionally activated to give almost only z-ions for de novo sequencing.
  • Acetylation-enhanced b-ion MALDI sequencing (2010): Acetylates tryptic peptides in methanol to raise b-ion intensity in MALDI TOF/TOF spectra, making the sequence ladders easier to read and validate. Applied to proteins of the unsequenced Antarctic bacterium Pseudomonas syringae.
  • High proton affinity N-terminal derivatization (2011): Guanidino and amidino N-terminal derivatization reagents that strengthen b-ion series under low-energy CID for easier de novo sequencing.
  • Oxazolone C-terminal derivatization (2011): Selective C-terminal derivatization, which is hard because C-terminal and side-chain carboxyls behave alike, improved in efficiency and given a bromine signature so the C-terminal fragment can be picked out and sequenced.
  • Br-signature N-terminal derivatization (2012): A MALDI signal-enhancing N-terminal derivatization carrying a bromine isotope signature, so the tagged N-terminal peptide is unmistakable in the spectrum. Used to read N-terminal processing such as methionine or signal-peptide removal, and to annotate uncharacterized proteins.
  • 193 nm UVPD of imidazolinylated Lys-N peptides (2012): Imidazolinylation of Lys-N peptides followed by 193 nm ultraviolet photodissociation, which yields a SINGLE product ion series instead of the overlapping series that defeat most de novo strategies.
  • CNBr ring-opening of cyclic OBOC peptides (2013): Places methionine in the macrocycle and linker so cyanogen bromide linearises and releases cyclic library peptides, which are then sequenced manually and with de novo software.
  • 351 nm UVPD de novo sequencing (2013): N-terminal chromophore derivatisation makes a peptide absorb at 351 nm, so selective UV photodissociation cleaves it into a simplified y-ion series instead of the mixed ladders CID gives, deliberately to make the spectra amenable to de novo sequencing.
  • 14N/15N Aba charge derivatization (2013): Derivatizes peptide N-termini with 14N- or 15N-labeled 4-amidinobenzoic acid so b-ions show as doublets and improved CID fragmentation simplifies de novo sequencing.
  • Br-TEMPO FRIPS de novo sequencing (2015): Adds bromine to the free-radical-initiated peptide sequencing reagent so N-terminal fragments carry a doublet signature that eases de novo reading.
  • Combinatorial acetylation/guanidinylation labeling (2017): Combined acetylation and guanidinylation of peptides gives equivalent b- and y-ion representation in ion-trap MS/MS, allowing accurate de novo annotation.
  • Carbamylation-assisted de novo sequencing (2018): Studies carbamylation and dual modification of peptides to enrich b- and y-ion series, including b1, for easier de novo sequencing by Q-TOF.
  • Scorpion venom chemical-modification de novo (2019): Chemical modification-assisted de novo sequencing of antimicrobial peptides in scorpion venom.
  • N-terminal imine derivatization (2020): Studies the CID fragmentation of N-terminal peptide imines as a derivatization that enhances de novo peptide sequencing.
  • FBSA N-terminal derivatization (2023): N-terminal derivatization with 4-formyl-benzenesulfonic acid under charge-reduction conditions, so overlapping positive- and negative-mode spectra pinpoint the b-ion series for de novo sequencing.
  • Tetrazine-cyclized peptide UV ring-opening (2023): Designs cyclic peptides on solid phase around a tetrazine so that UV light opens the ring and releases them, turning a cyclic sequence – which tandem MS deconvolutes badly, and which most approved peptide drugs are – into a linear one that sequences de novo.
  • Bis-cysteine macrocycle chemical linearization (2023): Cleaves synthetic bis-Cys macrocyclic peptides at a defined backbone position by Edman chemistry so that mixtures from macrocycle libraries can be sequenced de novo by nLC-MS/MS.
  • Thioether macrocycle oxidative linearization (2024): Oxidising the thioether of a thioether-closed macrocyclic peptide makes collision-induced dissociation open the ring at one site, so the linearised peptide can be sequenced de novo; demonstrated on a randomised macrocycle library.
  • Me-Cou N-terminal derivatization de novo sequencing (2025): N-terminal derivatization with N-succinimidyl 7-methoxycoumarin-3-carboxylate (Me-Cou), which boosts low-mass b-ion yield so de novo sequencing can read the N-terminal residues, applied to untargeted peptidomics on LC-TIMS-qTOF/MS.
  • PSMtags (2025): Small-molecule peptide conjugates found by systematic screening that sharply increase b-ion fragment coverage, raising sequence-identification confidence and improving de novo sequencing. Also supports 9-plex stable-isotope multiplexing at ~240 samples/day, boosting protein data points roughly 6.5x over the unlabelled baseline in sensitive (low-input) proteomics.

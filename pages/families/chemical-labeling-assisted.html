Chemical labeling assisted

16 methods · 1997–2024

Chemical labeling assisted: Isotopic or mass-coded labels introduced before the mass spectrometer, so one ion series can be told from the other by a known mass shift instead of by inference. The problem is eased in the sample, not the algorithm.

Methods (16)

Oldest first, by the paper that describes each one.

  • Rapid QTOF de novo sequencing (1997): Nanoelectrospray, isotopic-labeling, and quadrupole/time-of-flight workflow for rapid de novo peptide sequencing.
  • 18O ion trap de novo sequencing (1998): Ion-trap de novo peptide sequencing method using 18O labeling to distinguish fragment ion series.
  • Isotopic N-terminal labeling de novo sequencing (2000): Isotopic N-terminal labeling with a fragmentation-directing moiety for protein quantitation and facilitated de novo sequencing.
  • Differential methyl esterification labeling (2001): Labels peptide carboxyl groups with d0- or d3-methanol so that mixed samples give quantitation and isotope-paired fragment ions that support automated de novo sequencing.
  • MCAT (2002): Mass-coded abundance tagging strategy for de novo peptide sequencing and quantitative profiling of complex protein mixtures.
  • Amino acid-coded mass tagging (2002): Codes amino acids by mass before MALDI TOF/TOF so that incomplete CID of singly and doubly charged precursors still yields assignable series, recovering spectra that would otherwise go unidentified and making the instrument’s throughput usable for de novo work.
  • 15N-labeled 2D mass spectra (2004): Overlays spectra of 15N-labeled and unlabeled peptides so each ion carries its nitrogen count, adding discriminating power for identification and de novo interpretation.
  • Dual-labeling (guanidination/amidination) de novo sequencing (2005): Derivatizes peptides by guanidination and amidination so mass-coded labels distinguish N- and C-terminal fragment ions and promote informative fragmentation for de novo sequencing.
  • Reductive methylation de novo sequencing (2005): Reductive isotopic methylation strategy for de novo sequencing of neuropeptides.
  • Validator (18O pair picking) (2009): Finds 16O/18O-labeled peptide pairs and compares their fragmentation, validating Mascot results and, in Validator 3, deriving peptides from labeled y-ion pairs independently of the search engine.
  • Dimethyl isotope labeling de novo sequencing (2010): Dimethyl isotope labeling-assisted workflow for de novo peptide sequencing.
  • Asp-Pro linker 18O OBOC peptide sequencing (2012): Releases one-bead-one-compound hexapeptides through an acid-labile Asp-Pro linker in partly 18O water, so N- and C-terminal ions can be told apart during de novo sequencing.
  • NeuCode y-ion annotation (2013): Two lysine isotopologues differing by 36 mDa are embedded metabolically, so every C-terminal fragment appears as a resolvable doublet and N-terminal ones do not. An algorithm reads that signature to label y-type ions at 93.2% accuracy, which among other uses lifts correct de novo identifications with PepNovo+ by 34%.
  • Simplified 18O labeling de novo sequencing (2014): Adds H2 18O and trypsin directly to the digest so 18O is incorporated at peptide C-termini, marking y-ions to ease de novo sequencing without extra drying steps.
  • NIPTL-Novo (2017): Non-isobaric peptide termini labeling assisted peptide de novo sequencing.
  • R-met C-terminal labeling de novo sequencing (2024): Labels the peptide C-terminus and Asp/Glu carboxyl groups with arginine methyl ester so non-tryptic peptides fragment well, and EThcD then resolves isobaric ambiguities for bottom-up de novo protein sequencing.
